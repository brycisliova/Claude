Help and guides

Helplines first. Then calm, practical guides for the situations people most often find themselves in.

If you're in danger or thinking of ending your life

Call 112 for emergencies, or Tele-MANAS 14416, the government's free 24/7 mental health line in English and 20 Indian languages. You can also go to the nearest hospital emergency department. Whatever is happening, people want to help you through it.

It's okay not to know yet

Questioning is normal and has no deadline. Some people know early; others discover themselves in their twenties, forties or later.

  1. Give yourself time. Labels are tools, not boxes. Use one if it helps; change it if it stops fitting.
  2. Read and listen. Stories on Gaysi Family, Orinam and Agents of Ishq can help you feel less alone.
  3. Talk to someone safe. An affirming counsellor at iCALL or Humsafar can help you sort through feelings without judgement.
  4. Remember the science. Whatever you discover is not an illness and not your fault.

Your safety comes first

Coming out is your decision and your timing. Staying private to stay safe is not cowardice.

  1. Assess safety. Do you depend on this person for housing or money? Have they said hostile things? If so, consider waiting or building support first.
  2. Start with one safe person. A friend or sibling likely to be supportive can become an ally for harder conversations.
  3. Have a backup plan. Know where you could stay, keep documents accessible, save helpline numbers.
  4. Expect a process. Many families react badly at first and come around over months or years.
  5. Know you're protected. Adults in India have the right to live with a partner of their choice. If family threatens or confines you, contact the police, a lawyer or an NGO like Humsafar.

If your child just came out to you

They trusted you with something enormous. Your first reaction will be remembered for life.

  1. Say you love them. That sentence protects them more than anything else you can do.
  2. Don't look for a cure. Doctors who try to change orientation can face misconduct action in India, and research links such attempts to depression and suicide.
  3. Don't force marriage. It destroys two lives and has pushed queer people to suicide.
  4. Learn alongside them. The science and myths pages here are a good start. Parent support groups exist through Humsafar and Sappho for Equality.
  5. Feel your feelings elsewhere. It's okay to grieve the future you imagined. Talk to a counsellor or friend, not at your child.

How to be a good friend

  1. Keep secrets. Never out someone. Their story is theirs.
  2. Speak up when they're not there. Push back on jokes and slurs in group chats. Silence is read as agreement.
  3. Use the names and pronouns people ask for. If you slip, correct yourself briefly and move on.
  4. Correct myths with facts. Share the specific study that answers the claim.
  5. Check in. "How are you really doing?" can matter more than you know.

On campus and at work

  1. Look for queer collectives. Many Indian colleges, including the IITs, IIMs and central universities, have student-run queer groups. They're often the fastest route to friends who understand.
  2. Use the counselling centre. Campus counsellors are bound by confidentiality. If one isn't affirming, you can ask for another or use iCALL.
  3. Report harassment. Bullying and mocking are not "jokes." Colleges have anti-ragging and grievance cells; workplaces have HR and, for trans employees, protections under the 2019 Act.
  4. Check employer policies. Many Indian companies now offer partner benefits and employee resource groups.
  5. Document everything. Save screenshots and dates if you're being targeted.

What Indian law says

  1. Same-sex relationships are legal. Since Navtej Singh Johar (2018), consensual adult relationships are not a crime.
  2. Privacy covers orientation. Puttaswamy (2017) recognises sexual orientation as part of the fundamental right to privacy.
  3. Transgender people are legally recognised. NALSA (2014) and the 2019 Act prohibit discrimination. The 2026 amendment changed how identity certificates are issued; check the latest rules or ask a legal aid group.
  4. Conversion therapy by doctors is misconduct. You can complain to your State Medical Council.
  5. Same-sex marriage is not yet legal. The Supreme Court left it to Parliament in 2023.
