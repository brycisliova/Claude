Part one

What India counts, and what it does not

Anyone who gives you an exact yearly count of LGBTQ hate killings in India is guessing. Here is what the official tables actually hold.

No national hate-crime data. On 21 December 2021 the Home Ministry told the Lok Sabha that NCRB collected data on mob lynching and hate crimes in 2017, found it unreliable because the crimes are not defined, and discontinued collection. In December 2022 the Home Ministry told the Rajya Sabha that NCRB does not maintain data on hate crimes because the term is not defined in the IPC (ThePrint, BOOM).

The only LGBTQ-specific figures in the National Crime Records Bureau tables are for transgender people, and they count victims, not reasons. They say nothing about motive, and nothing at all about lesbian, gay or bisexual victims. A death caused by someone's identity is not a category in these tables.

Section 377 cases, before and after

Before 2018, Section 377 of the Indian Penal Code was used in cases of "unnatural offences". NCRB's counts include cases involving children, so they do not measure prosecutions of consenting adults. The Supreme Court noted in 2013 that fewer than 200 people had been prosecuted under it in over 150 years (Koushal judgment).

PIB Backgrounder, Rights of Transgender Persons in India

In 2026 the law on transgender identity changed

The Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Amendment Act, 2026 received assent on 30 March 2026 and came into force on 25 May 2026 (Supreme Court Observer). According to PRS Legislative Research, the bill removes trans-man, trans-woman and genderqueer from the statutory definition, retains socio-cultural identities and intersex variations, adds 'eunuch', and excludes persons with different sexual orientations and self-perceived sexual identities. Under it, the District Magistrate issues the identity certificate after examining the recommendation of a designated medical board headed by a Chief Medical Officer or Deputy Chief Medical Officer. The Act also deleted the 2019 Act's clause on the right to a self-perceived gender identity (Supreme Court Observer), and adds new offences, including forcing a person to present as transgender for begging (PRS summary).

The Supreme Court issued notice on the challenge to the Act on 4 May 2026 (Supreme Court Observer). On 17 August 2026 the Centre told it that identity cards already issued would continue (LiveLaw). In October 2025 the Court had said the 2019 Act and Rules had been "reduced to dead letters" through state indifference (Supreme Court Observer).

What the reports on suicide show. The 2025 review of 164 news reports (2001 to 2022) found the death was described in 88% of them, and the reasons most often framed were family non-acceptance (47%) and forced separation or relationship breakdown. The most common ages were 19 to 24 (36%) and 25 to 44 (32%). The author stresses these are news narratives, not rates (Khuman, 2025). Honour killings are sometimes disguised as suicides, so the true toll may be higher.

Part seven

Rejection costs lives. Acceptance saves them.

The same research that documents harm also shows what protects people.

  • US young adults who faced high family rejection were 8.4 times more likely to have attempted suicide (Ryan et al., 2009).
  • Young people in very accepting communities attempted suicide at less than half the rate of those in very unaccepting ones (Trevor Project).
  • US states that legalised same-sex marriage saw a 7% fall in high-school suicide attempts (Raifman et al., 2017).

Method

How this page was made, and what it cannot tell you

  • Every figure is linked. Each number comes from a document someone opened and read: a government table, a court judgment, a peer-reviewed study, an NGO report or a news article. The sources page lists all 288 of them.
  • We say who was asked. Most Indian surveys use convenience or snowball samples through community organisations. Their percentages describe the people surveyed, not all LGBTQ people in India.
  • Counts are minimums. Police, courts and media record only what is reported. Hate crime is not a category in Indian law, and in much of the world it is not counted.
  • Researcher counts are labelled. Where we counted rows in a published list (the India entries in Transgender Europe's lists), we say so.
  • What we left out. Anything we could not confirm in the original source. That includes official transgender crime figures before 2022, some Section 377 counts, the Pew 2019 figure for India, and the outcome of the 2026 Supreme Court hearings after 17 August 2026.
  • Last checked: 22 October 2026. Laws and numbers change. If you find an error or a newer figure, tell us and we will correct it.
