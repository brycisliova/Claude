Studying abroad

For LGBTQIA+ students, especially from India, who are thinking about a degree somewhere safer. Scholarships, how countries compare, what to ask before you go, and where to get help. Everything here was read on an official page, and we say so when we could not.

Read this first. Deadlines, rules and visa law change often. Everything below was checked on 23 October 2026. Open the official link before you decide anything, and treat this page as a map, not as advice from a lawyer or an adviser.

  • Very few scholarships are made for LGBTQ+ students from India. Most are for people already living in the US, UK or Australia. The real money is in general scholarships, and they do not ask about orientation.
  • You never have to share your orientation to get a place or a general scholarship. Write about it only if you want to.
  • Law is only part of safety. A strong score on a map does not mean every street is friendly. Look for a campus LGBTQ+ office or student group before you apply, and write to them.
  • Check partner rules first if you are in a relationship. On many courses a partner cannot join you, and the rules just changed in Australia.
  • Trans students: the rules in the US and the UK moved in 2025. Read each university's policy before you commit.

Scholarships made for LGBTQ+ students

Scholarships made for LGBTQ+ students

Awards made for LGBTQ+ students are few. Most are for people who already live or study in the US, UK or Australia, and many are closed right now. These are the ones we could confirm. Where an award is not open to Indian students, we say so.

Commonwealth Master's and PhD Scholarships through the Commonwealth Equality Network

United Kingdom · Closed now

Who it is for
People who work with or within LGBTI+ communities in low and middle income Commonwealth countries. You must be a citizen of, or have refugee status in, an eligible Commonwealth country. You must show need and explain how your study will support LGBTI+ rights. India is on the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission's Master's country list.
What you get
Full UK tuition fees, return airfare, a monthly living stipend and other study costs. The page names no amounts.
Good to know
The 2027 round has closed. It asks you to write about your LGBTI+ work, so think about privacy and safety first.

Inclusive Leaders Scholarship, Imperial College Business School

United Kingdom · Open for 2027-28

Who it is for
Outstanding LGBTQ+ leaders and advocates for diversity in business. New students on the Executive MBA, Weekend MBA or Global Online MBA. Home and overseas fee status are both listed.
What you get
Up to 30% of tuition for the Executive and Weekend MBA, and up to 40% for the Global Online MBA starting September 2027.
Good to know
These are MBA routes for people with work experience, not for first degrees or most Master's courses. Imperial also lists a Reaching Out MBA fellowship (below).

General scholarships open to Indian students

A one-year master's in the UK. The official page calls the awards fully funded.
Good to know
Chevening says plainly that sexual orientation does not matter in selection. Nothing in the process asks about it.

Which countries are safer and more protective

Which countries are safer and more protective

No ranking tells you how safe you will feel on an ordinary Tuesday. Law is one part. For Europe we use ILGA-Europe's 2026 Rainbow Map. Elsewhere we use laws and court rulings where we could open them, and a community-edited database where we could not. We say which is which.

Canada

Not on the Rainbow Map (Europe only) · Marriage since 2005

Protection in law
The Canadian Human Rights Act lists sexual orientation and gender identity or expression as prohibited grounds. It covers federally regulated bodies. Most universities, landlords and employers fall under provincial human rights laws, which we did not check. Equaldex (community edited) lists discrimination as illegal.
Trans students
Equaldex lists changing gender as 'varies by region'. Non-binary recognition is listed as recognised. We could not open an official page to check.
Before you choose
Rules on trans ID, health care and schools differ by province. Check the province before you choose a university.

laws-lois.justice.gc.ca · laws-lois.justice.gc.ca · pewresearch.org · equaldex.com · read 23 October 2026

Germany

7th of 49 on the Rainbow Map · Marriage since 2017

Protection in law
ILGA-Europe 2026 scores sexual orientation and gender identity as protected in employment and goods and services. Education protection is scored 0.44 out of 1, so only partly.
Trans students
ILGA-Europe 2026 scores Germany as having legal gender recognition based on self-determination, with non-binary recognition, and no medical or surgical requirement.
Before you choose
Protection in education is partial in the ILGA-Europe data. Check the state and university.

rainbowmap.ilga-europe.org · rainbowmap.ilga-europe.org · pewresearch.org · read 23 October 2026

Netherlands

13th of 49 on the Rainbow Map · Marriage since 2001

Protection in law
ILGA-Europe 2026 scores sexual orientation and gender identity as protected in employment, goods and services, and education.
Trans students
Legal gender recognition exists with no surgery or sterilisation required, and name change is available. ILGA-Europe does not score it as self-determination.
Before you choose
The ILGA-Europe data shows no national ban on conversion practices.

rainbowmap.ilga-europe.org · rainbowmap.ilga-europe.org · pewresearch.org · read 23 October 2026

United Kingdom

22nd of 49 on the Rainbow Map · Marriage since 2014 (England and Wales; Scotland 2014; Northern Ireland 2020)

Protection in law
The Equality Act 2010 lists sexual orientation and gender reassignment as protected characteristics. Section 91 covers universities and colleges in England, Wales and Scotland.
Trans students
On 16 April 2025 the Supreme Court held that 'man', 'woman' and 'sex' in the Equality Act mean biological sex. ILGA-Europe 2026 names the UK among six countries with explicit bans or other legal limits that make legal gender recognition practically impossible.
Before you choose
Trans students should read each university's policy first. The ruling and its guidance are still being applied.

legislation.gov.uk · legislation.gov.uk · supremecourt.uk · rainbowmap.ilga-europe.org · read 23 October 2026

Ireland

14th of 49 on the Rainbow Map · Marriage since 2015

Protection in law
ILGA-Europe 2026 scores sexual orientation as protected in employment, goods and services, education and health. It scores explicit gender identity protection in those areas as absent.
Trans students
Legal gender recognition is scored as self-determination, with no medical requirements.
Before you choose
ILGA-Europe says the Electoral Act bars international funding for campaigning or advocacy that tries to influence public policy.

rainbowmap.ilga-europe.org · rainbowmap.ilga-europe.org · pewresearch.org · irishstatutebook.ie · read 23 October 2026

Sweden

9th of 49 on the Rainbow Map · Marriage since 2009

Trans students
Sweden is the only country that passed new legal gender recognition law this year, but ILGA-Europe says it still falls short of self-determination and still treats trans identities as medical.
Before you choose
Legal gender recognition is not self-determination.

Before you choose
No national ban on conversion practices in the ILGA-Europe data.

Before you choose

Australia

Not on the Rainbow Map (Europe only) · Marriage since 2017

Trans students

Before you choose

Protection in law
Since Bostock (2020) employers cannot fire people for being gay or transgender under Title VII. Equaldex lists general discrimination and housing protections as varying by region.
Trans students

Before you choose
Trans and non-binary students face the biggest risk and uncertainty here. Rules and court cases keep changing. Ask the university and check a current legal source before you commit.

Who it is for

United States: January 2025 executive order on sex and identity documents

United States

Who it is for
Trans and non-binary students planning to study in the US.
What it says
The order says it is US policy to recognise two sexes, male and female, and that they are not changeable. It tells the State and Homeland Security departments to require that ID documents, including passports and visas, reflect the holder's sex as the order defines it.
Good to know
This is a real safety and paperwork caveat for trans students. Rules and court cases keep changing. Check the university's international office and a current legal source before you commit.

Student visas and bringing a partner

Student visas and bringing a partner

Rules for bringing a partner are tight and keep changing. On many taught Master's courses a partner cannot join you at all. Several of these pages never use the words same-sex, so if you are unsure, ask the immigration office before you pay for anything.

UK Student visa: partner and children as dependants

United Kingdom

Who it is for
Students on a PhD or research based higher degree starting on or after 1 January 2024, or on a government sponsored course longer than 6 months.
What it says
A dependant can be your husband, wife, civil partner or unmarried partner. The wording is not limited by sex. Each person needs money in the bank: £845 a month for London or £680 outside London, for up to 9 months.
Good to know
For most taught Master's students on the page's rules, partners cannot come. The page does not say 'same-sex' in so many words.

Australia: including family members in a Student visa

Australia

Who it is for
Student visa applicants who want to bring a partner or children.
What it says
You can no longer include a partner or children unless an exemption applies. Exemptions include PhD students, DFAT or Defence sponsored students, foreign government scholarship recipients, and eligible students from Pacific and ASEAN countries. Family cannot be added after a visa is granted.
Good to know
This is a big change. Do not assume your partner can follow you to Australia on a taught degree. India is not in the list of country exemptions shown.

In India

In India: documents and community groups

India's rules for transgender identity certificates were amended in 2026, and we could not confirm every detail from an official page. The community groups below mostly work on health and support. We did not find one that gives study-abroad advice.

Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Amendment Bill, 2026 (PRS summary)

India

Who it is for
Transgender and intersex people in India who need an identity certificate.
What it says
PRS says the Bill passed the Lok Sabha on 24 March and the Rajya Sabha on 25 March 2026. It adds that the District Magistrate issues the certificate after looking at a designated medical board's recommendation. It says certificate holders can change their first name in the birth certificate and other official documents.
Good to know
This changes the 2019 process. PRS also says the Bill drops trans man, trans woman and genderqueer from the listed categories. News reports we saw say the President gave assent on 30 March 2026. We did not confirm that, the commencement date, or any court case from an official page. Get current advice before applying.

Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019 (PRS summary)

India

Who it is for
Transgender people in India.
What it says
The 2019 Act lets a person apply to the District Magistrate for a certificate of identity as 'transgender'. It bars discrimination in education and employment. PRS says the Bill passed Parliament in November 2019.
Good to know
This describes the 2019 Bill as passed. The 2026 amendment above changes parts of it.
