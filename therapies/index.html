Ways to heal

Therapies and self-care

What the evidence says helps, explained simply, plus tools you can use right now. These support professional care; they don't replace it.

Ten approaches

Rated by how strong the evidence is

LGBTQ-affirmative therapy Strong consensus

Therapy that treats your identity as valid rather than a problem, and helps with the stress of stigma, family conflict and hiding. Every major professional body recommends this approach.

American Psychological Association practice guidelines for sexual minority clients.

Affirmative CBT Trial evidence

Cognitive behavioural therapy adapted for minority stress: challenging internalised shame, building assertiveness, reducing avoidance. The ESTEEM programme reduced depression and alcohol use in gay and bisexual men.

Pachankis et al., 2015, Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology.

Cognitive behavioural therapy Strong evidence

One of the best-studied treatments for depression and anxiety. Teaches you to spot and reshape unhelpful thought patterns.

Many meta-analyses and clinical guidelines.

Mindfulness and meditation Moderate evidence

Paying attention to the present without judgement. A JAMA review found meditation programmes produced moderate improvements in anxiety and depression.

Goyal et al., 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine.

Peer support and community Strong evidence

Connection with other queer people buffers minority stress. Support groups, college collectives and chosen family genuinely protect mental health.

Meyer, 2003; Hatzenbuehler, 2011.

Expressive writing Moderate evidence

Writing about painful experiences for 15 to 20 minutes over several days can improve mood and even physical health. Try it below.

Pennebaker, 1997, Psychological Science.

Music therapy Moderate evidence

Listening, singing or making music with a therapist. Probably reduces depression and anxiety in the short term. Try the sound studio.

Aalbers et al., 2017, Cochrane Review.

Physical activity Strong evidence

Walking, dancing, yoga or sport. Regular movement reliably reduces depressive symptoms, sometimes as much as medication for mild depression.

Multiple meta-analyses.

Art therapy Emerging evidence

Drawing, painting or making things to express what words can't. Promising, though studies are smaller. Try light painting or the kaleidoscope in the arcade.

Smaller trials and reviews.

Time in nature Emerging evidence

Green spaces, sunlight and fresh air are linked to lower stress and better mood.

Observational and experimental studies.

Finding an affirming therapist. Ask directly: "Do you work with LGBTQ+ clients, and do you see my identity as a healthy variation, not a problem?" iCALL and the Humsafar Trust can point you to affirming counsellors. A good therapist will welcome the question.

Right now

5-4-3-2-1 grounding

When anxiety spikes, bring yourself back to the present. Tap each step as you notice the things around you.

Expressive writing

Write it out

Research by James Pennebaker found that writing about difficult feelings for 15 to 20 minutes over a few days can improve wellbeing. Nothing you type here leaves your device.

Daily check-in

How are you, really?

Noticing patterns is a first step. Your check-ins stay on this device only.
