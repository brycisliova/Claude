Research library

Every key paper, what it found, and its limitations. Filter by question or search by author, journal or topic. Open any row to read it.

Papers

1948Sexual Behavior in the Human MaleKinsey, Pomeroy & Martin · Book, W.B. SaundersNot a choice
What it found

Interviews with thousands of Americans showed same-sex attraction was far more common than assumed, and that sexuality varies along a spectrum.

Its limits

Samples were not random, so exact percentages are unreliable. Its lasting contribution was showing diversity, not precise rates.

Read the original ↗
1957The adjustment of the male overt homosexualEvelyn Hooker · Journal of Projective TechniquesNot an illness
What it found

Expert clinicians shown blind personality tests from 30 gay and 30 straight men couldn't tell them apart. Gay men were as psychologically healthy.

Its limits

Small sample of non-patient men from one city, but it was the first rigorous challenge to the illness model.

Read the original ↗
1991A genetic study of male sexual orientationBailey & Pillard · Archives of General PsychiatryNot a choice
What it found

Identical twins of gay men were much more likely to be gay than fraternal twins or adopted brothers, pointing to a genetic influence.

Its limits

Volunteers were recruited through ads, which likely inflated concordance. Later population studies found smaller but still real genetic effects.

Read the original ↗
1991A difference in hypothalamic structure between heterosexual and homosexual menSimon LeVay · ScienceNot a choice
What it found

A small brain region (INAH3) was on average smaller in gay men than in straight men.

Its limits

Only 41 post-mortem brains, many affected by AIDS. Important as a first clue; not proof on its own.

Read the original ↗
1993A linkage between DNA markers on the X chromosome and male sexual orientationHamer et al. · ScienceNot a choice
What it found

Reported a link between a region called Xq28 and male homosexuality in families with several gay brothers.

Its limits

Replication was mixed. A larger 2015 study (Sanders et al.) supported it, but the 2019 GWAS did not find it. Treat as unsettled.

Read the original ↗
1996Homosexuality in men and number of older brothersBlanchard & Bogaert · American Journal of PsychiatryNot a choice
What it found

Each older brother increased the odds of a man being gay. This fraternal birth order effect is one of the most replicated findings in the field.

Its limits

Explains only a portion of gay men. It is a population pattern, not a rule for individuals.

Read the original ↗
2003Prejudice, social stress, and mental health in LGB populationsIlan Meyer · Psychological BulletinNot an illnessMental health
What it found

Introduced the minority stress model: higher rates of mental health problems among LGB people are explained by stigma and discrimination, not orientation itself.

Its limits

A review and theoretical model; many later studies have since tested and supported it.

Read the original ↗
2006Biological versus nonbiological older brothers and men's sexual orientationAnthony Bogaert · PNASNot a choice
What it found

Only older brothers born to the same mother mattered, even if raised apart. Stepbrothers or adopted brothers raised together had no effect. Points to the womb, not upbringing.

Its limits

Observational data; the biological mechanism was proposed later.

Read the original ↗
2008PET and MRI show differences in cerebral asymmetry and functional connectivity between homo- and heterosexual subjectsSavic & Lindström · PNASNot a choice
What it found

Brain symmetry patterns of gay men resembled straight women's, and lesbians' resembled straight men's, in areas unlikely to be shaped by learning.

Its limits

90 participants. Brain differences can't by themselves say what caused them.

Read the original ↗
2009Family rejection as a predictor of negative health outcomes in LGB young adultsRyan et al. · PediatricsMental healthFamily
What it found

Young people facing high family rejection were about 8.4 times more likely to report suicide attempts than those facing little or none.

Its limits

Cross-sectional US data, so it shows strong association rather than proven cause.

Read the original ↗
2009Appropriate therapeutic responses to sexual orientationAmerican Psychological Association Task Force · APA reportChange efforts
What it found

A systematic review found no reliable evidence that efforts to change sexual orientation work, and evidence of harm.

Its limits

Most change-effort studies it reviewed were themselves low quality, which is part of its conclusion.

Read the original ↗
2010Genetic and environmental effects on same-sex sexual behavior: a population study of twins in SwedenLångström et al. · Archives of Sexual BehaviorNot a choice
What it found

About 3,800 twin pairs. Genes explained roughly a third of variation in men and a fifth in women; shared family environment explained almost none.

Its limits

Measures behaviour rather than identity, and same-sex behaviour was rare, so estimates have wide uncertainty.

Read the original ↗
2010US National Longitudinal Lesbian Family Study: psychological adjustment of 17-year-old adolescentsGartrell & Bos · PediatricsFamily
What it found

Teenagers raised by lesbian mothers since birth were rated higher in social and academic competence and lower in behaviour problems than a normative sample.

Its limits

Volunteer families, mostly well-off and white, so not representative.

Read the original ↗
2011The social environment and suicide attempts in LGB youthMark Hatzenbuehler · PediatricsMental health
What it found

LGB youth in more supportive social environments had a significantly lower risk of suicide attempts.

Its limits

One US state's data; county-level environment measures.

Read the original ↗
2015Genome-wide scan demonstrates significant linkage for male sexual orientationSanders et al. · Psychological MedicineNot a choice
What it found

In 409 pairs of gay brothers, found linkage to regions on chromosome 8 and Xq28.

Its limits

Linkage studies are coarse; the 2019 GWAS found different specific loci.

Read the original ↗
2015LGB-affirmative cognitive-behavioral therapy for young adult gay and bisexual menPachankis et al. · Journal of Consulting and Clinical PsychologyMental health
What it found

A therapy targeting minority stress (ESTEEM) reduced depression and alcohol use.

Its limits

Small pilot trial of 63 men; larger trials have followed.

Read the original ↗
2016Sexual orientation, controversy, and scienceBailey et al. · Psychological Science in the Public InterestNot a choiceChange efforts
What it found

A major review concluding there is considerably more evidence for non-social causes of sexual orientation than social ones, and no good evidence it can be changed by therapy or upbringing.

Its limits

A review, so it depends on the studies it summarises, which it critiques openly.

Read the original ↗
2017Difference-in-differences analysis of state same-sex marriage policies and adolescent suicide attemptsRaifman et al. · JAMA PediatricsMental health
What it found

After US states legalised same-sex marriage, high-school suicide attempts fell by about 7%, with larger drops among sexual minority students.

Its limits

Strong design for observational data, though it can't rule out every other change.

Read the original ↗
2017Music therapy for depressionAalbers et al. · Cochrane Database of Systematic ReviewsMental health
What it found

Music therapy added to usual care probably reduces depressive symptoms and anxiety in the short term.

Its limits

Low to moderate certainty; studies varied in quality.

Read the original ↗
2018Male homosexuality and maternal immune responsivity to the Y-linked protein NLGN4YBogaert et al. · PNASNot a choice
What it found

Mothers of gay sons, especially those with older brothers, had higher antibody levels to a male-specific brain protein, a possible mechanism for the older-brother effect.

Its limits

Small sample; needs replication.

Read the original ↗
2019Large-scale GWAS reveals insights into the genetic architecture of same-sex sexual behaviorGanna et al. · ScienceNot a choice
What it found

477,522 people. No single gay gene; many variants with small effects. Common variants together explained 8 to 25% of variation.

Its limits

Measured ever having a same-sex partner, mostly in people of European ancestry. Authors warned against using it to predict anyone's orientation.

Read the original ↗
2019An alternative hypothesis for the evolution of same-sex sexual behaviour in animalsMonk et al. · Nature Ecology & EvolutionNature
What it found

Proposes same-sex behaviour may have been part of animal life from early in evolution, rather than an anomaly needing special explanation.

Its limits

A theoretical paper that calls for more data.

Read the original ↗
2020Parent-initiated sexual orientation change efforts with LGBT adolescentsRyan et al. · Journal of HomosexualityChange effortsFamily
What it found

Young adults whose parents tried to change their orientation had higher depression and suicide attempt rates and lower education and income.

Its limits

Retrospective self-reports from one US sample.

Read the original ↗
2020Association between recalled exposure to gender identity conversion efforts and psychological distress and suicide attemptsTurban et al. · JAMA PsychiatryChange effortsMental health
What it found

Trans adults exposed to conversion efforts had higher odds of lifetime suicide attempts, especially if exposed before age 10.

Its limits

Cross-sectional survey data.

Read the original ↗
2020Sexual orientation change efforts, adverse childhood experiences, and suicide ideation and attemptBlosnich et al. · American Journal of Public HealthChange effortsMental health
What it found

Sexual minority adults who went through change efforts had nearly twice the odds of suicidal thoughts and higher odds of attempts, even after accounting for childhood adversity.

Its limits

Observational; US national sample.

Read the original ↗
2025LGBTQ+ suicides in Indian news media, 2001–2022Dialogues in Health · Dialogues in HealthMental healthFamily
What it found

Analysis of 164 Indian news reports: family non-acceptance and forced separation were the most common narratives; joint couple suicides were frequent.

Its limits

Based on news coverage, so it reflects what gets reported, not all cases.

How to read this library. One study never settles a question. Small or early studies (like LeVay 1991 or Hamer 1993) were important but had limits, and some findings didn't fully replicate. The conclusions on this site rest on what many independent studies, across countries and decades, keep finding.
